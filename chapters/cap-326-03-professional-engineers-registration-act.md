## PART III

Registration of Professional Engineers

*Register.*

**11.** (1) The Registrar shall keep a Register of Professional Engineers (hereinafter referred to as “the Register”) in which he shall cause to be entered the name of every person required by section 12 or entitled under section 13 (4) of this Act to be registered as a professional engineer together with the following particulars in relation to each such person,

(a) his full address;

(b) the date of his registration; and

(c) the description of his qualifications.

(2) The Register shall, at all reasonable times, be open to inspection at the Registrar’s office without payment of a fee.

(3) A person whose name is included in the Register shall as and when any change occurs in his qualifications or address, inform the Council of such change.

(4) The Council, if satisfied about the change in qualifications, shall send the information so received to the Registrar for making necessary changes in the Register.

(5) A copy of the Register duly certified by the Registrar to be a correct copy shall be prima facie evidence that any person named in the Register is registered as a professional engineer in accordance with the provisions of this Act.

*Registration of existing professional engineers.*

**12.** The Registrar shall, within three months after the commencement of this Act, cause to be entered in the Register, the particulars specified in section 11 (1) (a), (b) and (c) of this Act, of all those persons who immediately before such commencement were practicing as professional engineers in Belize, without payment of a fee, and pending such entry, such persons shall be deemed to be professional engineers within the meaning of this Act.

*Registration of new professional engineers.*

**13.** (1) For the purposes of this Act, there shall be appointed annually by the Council,

(a) a Board of Professional Engineering Education (hereinafter referred to as “the Board”) constituted in accordance with Regulations which may be made by the Council for this purpose; and

a Committee (hereinafter referred to as “the (b)

Admission Committee”) constituted in accordance with Regulations which may be made by the Council for this purpose.

(2) It shall be the duty of the Board to recommend to the Council,

(a) the recognition of any examinations in professional engineering the passing of which

ought, in the opinion of the Board, to qualify persons for registration under this Act;

(b) the holding of any examination in professional engineering which ought, in the opinion of the Board, to be passed by applicants for registration under this Act, and to hold examinations in professional engineering in accordance with this Act and any Regulations made under this Act.

(3) It shall be the duty of the Admission Committee to consider every application for registration under this Act and to report to the Council whether or not the applicant is, in the opinion of the Committee, fit and qualified for registration as a professional engineer.

(4) Any person who, after the commencement of this Act, applies to the Admission Committee in the prescribed manner to be registered as a professional engineer and who satisfies that Committee that he is,

(a) qualified to be so registered;

(b) of good character; and

(c) physically and mentally fit to practice professional engineering in Belize,

shall be entitled, on payment of the prescribed fee, to be registered as a professional engineer and to be elected to the membership of the Association.

(5) A person is qualified to be registered as a professional engineer under subsection (4) (a) of this section if he holds a diploma, degree, fellowship, membership licence, certificate, or other status or form of recognition or registration granted by

a University, College or Board empowered to confer authority to practice professional engineering by the law of the country or place where such diploma, degree, fellowship, membership, licence, certificate or other status is granted and which in the opinion of the Board is evidence of satisfactory professional engineering training.

(6) An applicant for registration shall furnish to the Committee,

(a) evidence of his professional qualifications and experience;

(b) proof of his identity;

(c) proof of current registration, if applicable; and

(d) such other information as the Committee may require.

(7) The Council shall cause a written notice of its decision on any application for registration under this section to be served on the applicant within the prescribed period after the date of the decision.

(8) Where the Board recommends to the Council the recognition of any diploma, degree, fellowship, membership, licence, certificate or other status under subsection (5) of this section, the Council shall recognise that qualification for the purposes of section 13 (4) (a) or subsection (4) (a) of this section.

*Certificate of registration.*

**14.** (1) If the Admission Committee recommends to the Council that an applicant is fit and qualified to be registered as a professional engineer, the Council shall, after receiving the prescribed registration fee from the applicant, authorise the

Registrar in writing to enter the name of the applicant in the Register, and the Registrar shall as soon as possible enter the name of the applicant in the Register and furnish the applicant with a certificate of registration in the prescribed form for professional engineering.

(2) Every person registered as a professional engineer under this Act shall be entitled to practice professional engineering in Belize and to demand and recover reasonable charge for services rendered by him as a professional engineer.

(3) A certificate of registration shall expire on the 31st of December in each year.

## PART IV

Miscellaneous

*Prohibition on practice without a licence.*

**15.** (1) No person shall engage in the practice of professional engineering or hold himself out as a professional engineer unless that person is registered under this Act.

(2) For the purpose of this section, proof of the performance of one act in the practice of professional engineering on one occasion is sufficient to establish that the person was engaging in the practice of professional engineering.

*Association may institute legal proceedings*

**16.** The Association may in its own name, by any officer of the Council or any other person duly authorised in that behalf under the hand of its President, institute, carry on, prosecute and defend any actions, complaint, information or proceedings whatever, and every court shall take judicial notice of the signature of the President of the Council to any such authorisation.

*Use of titles.*

Any registered person shall be entitled to take and use the name or title of “Registered Professional Engineer” but no person shall practice under any name, title or style containing the words “Registered Professional Engineer” unless he is registered under this Act, and any unregistered person who so practices or wilfully pretends to be a registered professional engineer, or takes or uses the name or title of a registered professional engineer or any name, title, style or description implying that he is a registered person, shall on summary conviction be liable to a fine not exceeding one thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding one year.

*Removal of name from Register for failure to notify present address*

**18.** For the purpose of maintaining the Register, the Council may at any time by notice in writing addressed to the last known address of any professional engineer as reflected in the Register, inquire if such professional engineer has changed his regular business address, and if no answer shall be received within six months from the sending of such notice, the Council shall again send to the professional engineer a further notice by post as a registered letter, and if no answer shall be received within three months from the sending of the further notice, it shall be lawful for the Council to recommend to the Registrar that he removes the name of the professional engineer from the Register.

*Offences and penalties.*

**19.** (1) Any person who procures or attempts to procure registration as a professional engineer for himself or any other person by making or producing or causing to be made or produced any false or fraudulent representation or declaration, either orally or in writing, commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding two years.

(2) Any person who, during a period when his registration as a professional engineer under this Act is suspended, or who, not being registered as a professional engineer, practices

professional engineering or any branch of professional engineering, or acts as a partner to any professional engineer commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding two years.

*General penalty provision*

**20.** Every person who contravenes any provision of this Act for which no other penalty is provided shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding two years.

*Recovery of penalties.*

**21.** All penalties and fees imposed by or pursuant to this Act may be recovered in a summary way by complaint.

*Time limit for proceedings*

**22.** Any proceedings under this Act may be instituted within twelve month after the offence is committed, or within twelve months after the discovery of the offence, whichever is the later period.

*Association to be governed by its Constitution.*

**23.** The Association shall be governed by its Constitution set out in the Schedule to this Act which Schedule shall be read and construed as an integral part of this Act.

*Regulations.*

**24.** (1) The Council may, after consultation with the members of the Association and with the approval of the Minister, make Regulations generally for giving effect to the purposes and provisions of this Act and in particular, but without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing, may make Regulations,

(a) prescribing any other requirements or criteria which shall be prerequisites for registration;

(b) prescribing the form of the Register and any notice or document required to be given or made under this Act;

(c) prescribing the level of fees payable under this Act;

(d) requiring the making of returns of information by members of the Association in respect of names, addresses, telephone numbers, professional partners, employees and areas of professional activity;

(e) requiring professional engineers to keep books and records of business transactions;

(f) prescribing a code of ethics for professional engineers;

(g) prescribing the type of buildings which shall only be constructed after having been supervised by a professional engineer registered under this Act;

(h) prescribing any other matter or thing which may be, or is required by this Act to be prescribed.

## SCHEDULE PROFESSIONAL ENGINEERS (REGISTRATION) ACT Constitution of the Association of Professional Engineers of Belize (Section 23)

ARTICLE I Name of Association The name of the association shall be the Association of Professional Engineers of Belize.

ARTICLE II Objectives of the Association The objects and purposes for which the Association of Professional Engineers of Belize (hereinafter called “the Association”) is hereby constituted are, (i) to promote and encourage the general advancement of the professions of Professional Engineers and Allied Professions (hereinafter called “the professions”);

(ii) to facilitate the exchange of information and ideas among the members of the Association;

(iii) to provide a means of evaluating the development of professional skills

among members of the professions and to provide professional status to those members of the professions who have exemplified themselves as worthy of such status in accordance with the terms and conditions laid down by this Constitution and its By-laws;

(iv) to regulate the conduct of members of the Association such as to encourage the application of the highest standards of excellence in the performance of services and in the ethics of the professions;

(v) to implement standards in the practice of the professions and instill a conscious recognition of the professions’ obligation to society;

(vi) to seek, for the protection and benefit of society, legislation for mandatory registration of engineers to practice in Belize.

### ARTICLE IV

Admission, Expulsion and Retirement

**1.** Candidates shall be admitted to membership in the Association only by the Council. A candidate elected shall be admitted as a Corporate Member, Associate Member, Affiliate Member or Student Member, as the case may be, on payment of such entrance fee or installment and annual subscription as the By-laws may prescribe. Should payment of such dues not be made within the period of three calendar months after the date of notification to the candidate of his election, such election may be declared null and void by the Council and the candidate shall be notified accordingly.

**2.** The Council in accordance with the By-laws may expel any member who shall have willfully acted in contravention of the By-laws and/or Code of Professional Conduct of the Association, or who, in the opinion of the Council, shall have been guilty of such conduct as shall render him unfit to continue to belong to the Association, and he shall thereupon cease to be an Honorary Member, Corporate Member, Associate Member, Affiliate Member or Student Member (as the case may be) of the Association.

**3.** Any member of any class may retire from the Association by sending his resignation in writing to the Secretary, after payment of all subscriptions due from him, including for that current year.

### ARTICLE V

Fees and Dues

An entrance fee in an amount determined by the Council for the class of membership to which the candidate is elected shall be due and payable as of the date of election to that class. The schedules of entrance fees and annual dues shall be established, and when established may be modified by a two-thirds favourable vote of the Council. The schedule of fees and dues shall be set forth in the By-laws.

### ARTICLE VI

Nomination and Election of Officers

**1.** The officers of the Association shall be a President, Vice-President, Secretary and Treasurer.

**2.** The Council shall consist of the President, Vice-President, Secretary, Treasurer and two other members. All members of the Council shall be Corporate Members.

**3.** The term of office of all elected officers shall be two years and the conditions of eligibility for re-election to the same office or any other office shall be as specified in the By-laws. A term of office shall begin at the close of the Annual General Meeting at which the Officer’s election is announced and shall continue until a successor is elected.

**4.** Vacancies in the offices of President, Vice-President, Secretary, Treasurer and Members of Council shall, subject to the provisions of the By-laws, be filled annually by a ballot of members of the Association.

In the case of disability of any other officer of the Association, or neglect in performance of his duties, the Council shall have power to declare the office vacant.

### ARTICLE VII

Management

**1.** The Council shall direct and manage the property and affairs of the Association in accordance with the Regulations and the By-laws of the Association and may exercise all such powers of the Association except those required to be exercised by the Association in General Meeting.

**2.** The detailed management of the affairs of the Association shall be exercised by the Secretary acting as general manager under authority of the Council.

### ARTICLE VIII

Meetings

**1.** General Meetings of the Association shall be held at such places and at such times as the Council may appoint.

**2.** The meetings of the Association shall be as follows,

(a) Ordinary Meetings for the reading and discussion of papers, for lectures and discussions, and for visits to works of interest;

(b) the Annual General Meetings;

(c) Special General Meetings.

All members shall be entitled to vote at the meetings specified in paragraph 2 of this Article, and each voting member shall have one vote.

**4.** In addition to the Ordinary Meetings specified by paragraph 2 (a), the Council may at their discretion arrange for other meetings to be held for the dissemination of knowledge by means of lectures or cinematograph, slide show or other pictorial representations, or for the discussion of papers; and the Council shall determine the conditions of admission to such meetings, and the manner in which they shall be conducted.

**5.** The Ordinary Meetings of the Association shall be conducted as prescribed by the Council from time to time and the Council shall determine the conditions of admission to such meetings.

**6.** At the Ordinary Meetings no question shall be discussed or motion made, relating to the direction and management of the Association.

**7.** Every member of any class shall have the privilege of introducing one or more visitors at each Ordinary Meeting of the Association, by writing his or their names in the book provided for that purpose.

**8.** The Annual General Meeting shall be held in the month of June or at such earlier time as the Council may determine. The business of the Annual General Meeting shall be to receive and consider the Report of the Council and the Accounts of the Association for the past year; to elect officers, and (with the approval of the Council) to transact any other business of which notice in writing shall have been given to the Secretary at least seven days before such meeting.

A Special General Meeting may be called at any time by the Council for any specific purpose relating to the direction and management of the affairs of the Association for amending the Constitution or any Bylaws of the Association and the Council shall at all times call such meeting on a requisition of five members specifying the nature of the business to be transacted.

### ARTICLE IX

Organization

**1.** In pursuance of the objects of the Association, the Council may at their discretion set up one or more committees of the Association as they may specify, for the holding of meetings, the reading of papers and for the discussion of subjects or for other activities falling within the purview of the Association, and the Council may dissolve any such committee at any time.

**2.** The Council may form such Branches and/or Divisions as may seem to them necessary in order to meet the needs of groups of members. The continuance of such Branches and/or Divisions and the terms of their relationship with the Council, and the rules under which they operate, shall be as the Council may determine under the By-laws.

### ARTICLE X

Amendments

**1.** A proposed amendment to the Constitution shall be submitted to the Secretary in written petitions signed by not less than five members of the Association. Counterparts of petitions may be signed and presented with the same effect as if the signature were on one petition.

Petitions for a proposed amendment shall be presented to the Secretary not less than twenty-eight days in advance of the date of the Annual General Meeting or Special General Meeting. A proposed amendment in accordance with the intent of the petitions shall be drafted by the Secretary and sent by letter to the members of the Association at least fourteen days in advance of the Annual General Meeting or Special General Meeting. The proposed amendment shall be presented for discussion at the Annual General Meeting or Special General Meeting and may be amended in any manner pertinent to the petitions by two-thirds vote,

Provided that, if the meeting shall so decide by majority vote, it may refer the proposed amendment to a committee for further consideration and a report at the next Annual General Meeting or Special General Meeting.

**3.** In a similar manner amendments to the By-laws of the Association may be proposed but only at the Special General Meeting convened for that purpose. Such amendments shall only be regarded as approved by the Association, if two-thirds or more of the members present in person or by proxy at such Special General Meeting vote in favour of such amendment.

### ARTICLE XI

Incorporation of Kindred Societies

The Council may, upon receipt of a request to the effect, from any Society with objects kindred to those of the Association, arrange for the union, alliance or incorporation of such Society with the Association and may also remit or reduce the entrance fees of the members of such Society at the time of their union, alliance or incorporation,

Provided that no such union, alliance or incorporation shall be effective unless it is sanctioned by a Special General Meeting.
