## PART I Preliminary

*Short title.*

**1.** This Act may be cited as the Mutual Legal Assistance and International Co-operation Act.

*Interpretation.*

**2.** (1) In this Act–

“Belizean ship” means a ship registered in Belize;

“cash” means–

(a) coins and notes in any currency;

(b) postal orders;

cheques of any kind, including travellers’ (c)

cheques;

bankers’ drafts;

(d)

(e) bearer bonds and bearer shares; and

(f) any kind of monetary instrument prescribed by order of the Minister responsible for finance as cash;

“central authority” means the central authority designated in section 4;

“Commissioner” means the Commissioner of Police;

“Convention State” means a State which is a party to the Vienna Convention;

“customs officer” means an officer appointed as a customs officer and includes the Comptroller of Customs;

“exported”, in relation to any cash, includes its being brought to any place in Belize for the purpose of being exported;

“evidence” includes documents and any other articles;

“information” means any fact, statement or record in any form that is relevant or material for the enforcement of any provision under this Part;

“Minister” means the Minister with responsibility for foreign affairs;

“police officer” means a member of the Belize Police Department;

“request” means a request for assistance by a requesting State to a requested State, which is made in accordance this Act;

“requested State” means a State that receives a request under this Act;

“requesting State” means a State that makes a request under this Act;

“Schedule” means a schedule to this Act;

“ship” includes any vessel used in navigation;

“State” includes reference to any territory;

“treaty” means a treaty or agreement that provides for mutual legal assistance, including the exchange of information and the performance and enforcement of specified requirements with respect to legal assistance in criminal matters, entered into by the Government of Belize with the government of another State; and

“Vienna Convention” means the United Nations Convention on Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances signed in Vienna on 20th December, 1988.

(2) If, in any proceedings under this Act, any question arises whether any State is a Convention State or is a party to the Vienna Convention, or any other treaty, convention or agreement, a certificate issued by or under the authority of the Minister shall be conclusive evidence on that question.

*Application and scope.*

**3.** (1) Subject to sub–section (5), this Act regulates the rendering of assistance by Belize to any foreign State.

(2) Assistance under this Act may be provided to any foreign State whether on the basis of a treaty or not.

(3) Notwithstanding sub–section (1), nothing precludes Belize from rendering of a broader range of assistance to a foreign State under this Act than may be provided for in a treaty.

(4) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to limit the power conferred under any other law to any agency or authority in Belize to co–operate, including sharing information, with any foreign State through other channels or in another manner.

(5) Where a request is made pursuant to a treaty, the provisions of that treaty apply.

*Central Authority: making and receiving requests.*

**4.** (1) For the purposes, a central authority is established and the Attorney General is designated as the central authority.

(2) The central authority shall exercise such functions as are conferred upon him by this Act, including the following–

(a) to make and receive requests and to execute, or arrange for the execution of, requests;

(b) where necessary, to certify or authenticate, or arrange for the certification and authentication of, any documents or other material supplied in response to a request;

(c) to take practical measures to facilitate the orderly and expeditious disposition of requests;

(d) to communicate and agree on conditions related to requests and to take such measures as necessary to ensure compliance with those conditions;

(e) to make any arrangements considered necessary to transmit any material gathered in response to a request to the competent authority of the requesting State or to authorize any other agency or authority to do so; and

(f) to carry out such other functions as provided for by this Act or which may be–

necessary for effective assistance to be provided or received; or

(ii) prescribed.

(3) In the case of a request received by an agency or authority in Belize other than the central authority–

(a) the request shall be referred as soon as possible to the central authority;

(b) the request, or action taken in response to it, is not invalid only for the reason that the central authority did not receive the request directly from the foreign State; and

(c) a Magistrate or a Judge may not reject the request on the grounds that the central authority did not receive it directly from the foreign State.

*Spontaneous transmission of information.*

**5.** Nothing in this Act shall prevent the central authority of Belize or any other agency or authority in Belize, from, without prior request, spontaneously transmitting information relating to a criminal matter to a central authority or other agency or authority that performs similar functions in a foreign State.

## PART II

General Provisions

*Form of request.*

**6.** (1) The central authority may accept a written request from a foreign State where the written request complies with this section.

(2) For the purposes of this section, a written request shall–

bear the original signature and seal of a person authorised by the foreign State to make a request; and

(b) not be transmitted by electronic means.

(3) In urgent cases, the central authority may accept an oral request, or a request communicated by facsimile or e–mail, provided it is confirmed by a written request within seven days.

*Content of request.*

**7.** (1) A request shall include–

(a) the name of the authority conducting the investigation, prosecution or judicial proceeding to which the request relates, including contact details of the person capable of responding to enquiries concerning the request;

(b) a description of the criminal matter, including a summary of the facts and, if applicable, offences and penalties concerned;

(c) a description of the purposes of the request, including reference to the legal basis on which the request is made, as well as the nature of the assistance sought.

(2) If the information set forth in sub–section (1) is not sufficient, Belize may request the foreign State to provide additional information.

(3) Notwithstanding sub–section (1), the fact that a request for assistance does not contain the information set out therein shall not affect the validity of the request or preclude its execution.

**8.** (1) A request shall be executed in accordance with any

*Application of specific procedures sought by the requesting state.*

procedures specified in the request, unless such execution would be contrary to the fundamental principles of the laws of Belize.

(2) For greater certainty, sub–section (1) applies even if the requested procedures are not used in Belize or are not available domestically in relation to the type of assistance sought.

*General provision.*

**9.** If a foreign State requests a form of assistance not specifically mentioned in this Act but available under the laws of Belize for domestic criminal matters, the assistance sought may be provided, unless declined by the central authority in accordance with section 10, to the same extent and under the same conditions as would be available to law enforcement authorities of Belize in a domestic criminal matter.

*Grounds for declining request.*

**10.** (1) Notwithstanding section 37, the central authority may, where he determines that the costs of responding to a request may exceed five hundred dollars, decline the request if the requesting State does not agree to pay the difference in the costs of responding to the request, whether incurred by the central authority or any other person.

(2) The central authority may also decline a request if–

(a) the request is not in writing;

(b) the request is not written in, or accompanied by a certified translation into, English;

(c) Belize makes a request for additional information under section 7(2) and does not receive a response within thirty days of the date of Belize’s request;

(d) with respect to information relating to an offence which is a summary offence under the

laws of Belize, the requested information relates to a period that is more than twelve months after the offence was committed;

(e) the request pertains to information in the possession or control of a person other than a person named in the request or it does not relate specifically to the affairs of the person named in the request;

(f) the requested information is protected from disclosure under the laws of Belize on the grounds of legal professional privilege;

(g) the requesting State would not be able to obtain the requested information–

(i) under its own laws for the purposes of the administration or enforcement of its criminal laws; or

(ii) in response to a valid request from the central authority under this Act;

(h) in the opinion of the central authority, granting the request would prejudice the sovereignty, security, or be contrary to public policy of, Belize; or

(i) the central authority is not satisfied that the requesting State will keep the information confidential and will not disclose it to any person other than–

(i) a person or authority in its own jurisdiction for the purposes of the

administration and enforcement of its criminal laws; or

(ii) a person employed or authorized by the government of the requesting State to oversee data protection.

*Notice of refusal.*

**11.** Where the central authority declines, in whole or in part, a request under this Part, he shall notify the requesting State and give reasons for his decision.

Mutual Service of Process

*Service of overseas process in Belize.*

**12.** (1) This section has effect where the central authority receives from the government of, or other authority in, a foreign State–

(a) a summons or other process requiring a person to appear as a defendant or attend as a witness in criminal proceedings in that State; or

(b) a document issued by a court exercising criminal jurisdiction in that State and recording a decision of the court made in exercise of that jurisdiction,

together with a request for that process or document to be served on a person in Belize.

(2) The central authority may cause the process or document to be served by post or, if the request is for personal service, cause it to be personally served on the person concerned.

(3) Service by virtue of this section of any such process or document as is mentioned in sub–section (1) does not impose any obligation under the laws of Belize to comply with it.

(4) Any such process or document served by virtue of this section shall be accompanied by a notice–

(a) stating the effect of sub–section (3);

(b) indicating that the person on whom it is served may wish to seek advice as to the possible consequences of his failing to comply with the process or document under the law of the State where it was issued; and

(c) indicating that under that law he may not, as a witness, be accorded the same rights and privileges as he would be accorded in criminal proceedings in Belize.

*Service of Belizean process overseas.*

**13.** (1) Process of the following descriptions, that is to say–

(a) a summons requiring a person charged with an offence to appear before a court in Belize; or

(b) a summons or order requiring a person to attend before a court in Belize for the purpose of giving evidence in criminal proceedings;

may be issued or made notwithstanding that the person in question is outside Belize and may be served outside Belize in accordance with arrangements made by the central authority.

(2) Service of any process outside Belize by virtue of this section does not impose any obligation under the law of Belize to comply with it, and accordingly failure to comply with any such process does not constitute contempt of any court nor is it a ground for issuing a warrant to secure the attendance of the person in question.

(3) Sub–section (2) is without prejudice to the service of any process, with the usual consequences for noncompliance, on the person in question if subsequently effected in Belize.

Mutual Provision of Evidence

*Overseas evidence for use in Belize.*

**14.** (1) Where, on an application made in accordance with sub–section (2), it appears to a Magistrate or Judge–

(a) that an offence has been committed or that there are reasonable grounds for suspecting that an offence has been committed; and

(b) that proceedings in respect of the offence have been instituted or that the offence is being investigated,

he may issue a letter (a “letter of request”) requesting assistance in obtaining outside Belize such evidence as is specified in the letter for use in the proceedings or investigation.

(2) An application under sub–section (1) may be made by the central authority or, if proceedings have been instituted, by the person charged in those proceedings.

(3) The central authority may himself issue a letter of request if–

(a) he is satisfied as to the matters mentioned in paragraph (1)(a); and

(b) the offence in question is being investigated or he has instituted proceedings in respect of it.

(4) Subject to sub–section (5), a letter of request shall be sent to the Minister for transmission either–

to a court or tribunal specified in the letter and exercising jurisdiction in the place where the evidence is to be obtained; or

(b) to an authority recognised by the government of the State in question as the appropriate authority for receiving requests for assistance of the kind to which this section applies.

(5) In case of urgency, a letter of request may be sent direct to such a court or tribunal as is mentioned in paragraph (4)(a).

(6) Evidence obtained by virtue of a letter of request shall not without the consent of such an authority as is mentioned in paragraph (4)(b) be used for any purpose other than that specified in the letter; and, when any document or other article obtained pursuant to a letter of request is no longer required for the purpose specified in the letter, or for any other purpose for which such consent has been obtained, it shall be returned to such an authority unless that authority indicates that the document or article need not be returned.

(7) In exercising any discretion conferred by law to exclude evidence in relation to a statement to which this sub–section applies, the court shall have regard–

(a) to whether it was possible to challenge the statement by questioning the person who made it; and

(b) if proceedings have been instituted, to whether the local law allowed the parties to the proceedings to be legally represented when the evidence was being taken.

(8) Sub–section (7) applies to a statement contained in evidence taken pursuant to a letter of request.

This section has effect where the central authority

*Belizean evidence for use overseas.*

receives–

(a) from a court or tribunal exercising jurisdiction in a foreign State or a prosecuting authority in such a State; or

(b) from another authority in such a State which appears to him to have the function of making requests of the kind to which this section applies,

a written request for assistance in obtaining evidence in Belize in connection with criminal proceedings that have been instituted, or a criminal investigation that is being carried on, in that State.

(2) If the central authority is satisfied–

(a) that an offence under the law of the State in question has been committed or that there are reasonable grounds for suspecting that such an offence has been committed; and

(b) that proceedings in respect of that offence have been instituted in that State or that an investigation into that offence is being carried on there,

he may, if he thinks fit, by a notice in writing nominate a court in Belize to receive such of the evidence to which the request relates as may appear to that court to be appropriate for the purpose of giving effect to the request.

(3) For the purpose of satisfying himself as to the matters mentioned in paragraphs (2)(a) and (b), the central authority

shall regard as conclusive a certificate issued by such authority in the State in question as appears to him to be appropriate.

*Schedule I.*

(4) The Schedule I has effect with respect to the proceedings before a nominated court in pursuance of a notice under sub– section (2).

*Transfer of prisoner held in Belize to give evidence or assist investigation overseas. CAP. 139.*

**16.** (1) The central authority may, if he thinks fit, issue a warrant providing for any person (a “prisoner”) serving a sentence in a prison or other institution to which the Prisons Act, applies to be transferred to a foreign State for the purpose–

(a) of giving evidence in criminal proceedings there; or

(b) of being identified in, or otherwise by his presence assisting, such proceedings or the investigation of an offence.

(2) No warrant shall be issued under this section in respect of a prisoner unless he has consented to being transferred as mentioned in sub–section (1), and that consent may be given either–

(a) by the prisoner himself; or

(b) in circumstances in which it appears to the central authority inappropriate, by reason of the prisoner’s physical or mental condition or his youth, for him to act for himself, by a person appearing to the central authority to be an appropriate person to act on his behalf;

but a consent once given is not capable of being withdrawn after the issue of the warrant.

(3) The effect of a warrant under this section is to authorise–

the taking of the prisoner to a place in Belize and his delivery at a place of departure from Belize into the custody of a person representing the appropriate authority of the State to which the prisoner is to be transferred; and

(b) the bringing of the prisoner back to Belize and his transfer in custody to the place where he is liable to be detained under the sentence to which he is subject.

(4) Where a warrant has been issued in respect of a prisoner under this section, he shall be deemed to be in legal custody at any time when, being in Belize or on board a Belizean ship or an aircraft registered in Belize, he is being taken under the warrant to or from any place or being kept in custody under the warrant.

(5) A person authorised by or for the purposes of the warrant to take the prisoner to or from any place or to keep him in custody has all the powers, authority, protection and privileges of a police officer.

(6) If the prisoner escapes or is unlawfully at large, he may be arrested without warrant by a police officer and taken to any place to which he may be taken under the warrant issued under this section.

(7) This section applies to a person in custody awaiting trial or sentence and a person committed to prison for default in paying a fine as it applies to a prisoner, and the reference in paragraph (3)(b) to a sentence shall be construed accordingly.

This section has effect where–

*Transfer of overseas prisoner to give evidence or assist investigation in Belize.*

(a) a witness summons or writ of subpoena has been issued in criminal proceedings in Belize in respect of a person (a “prisoner”) who is detained in custody in a foreign State by virtue of a sentence or order of a court or tribunal exercising criminal jurisdiction in that State; or

(b) it appears to the central authority that it is desirable for a prisoner to be identified in, or otherwise by his presence to assist, such proceedings or the investigation in Belize of an offence.

(2) If the central authority is satisfied that the appropriate authority in the State where the prisoner is detained will make arrangements for him to come to Belize to give evidence pursuant to the witness summons or writ of subpoena or, as the case may be, for the purpose mentioned in paragraph (1)(b), he may issue a warrant under this section.

(3) A warrant shall not be issued under this section in respect of any prisoner unless he has consented to being brought to Belize to give evidence as aforesaid or, as the case may be, for the purpose mentioned in paragraph (1)(b), but a consent once given is not capable of being withdrawn after the issue of the warrant.

(4) The effect of the warrant is to authorise–

(a) the bringing of the prisoner to Belize;

(b) the taking of the prisoner to, and his detention in custody at, such place or places in Belize as are specified in the warrant; and

the returning of the prisoner to the State from which he has come.

(5) Sections 16(4) to (7) have effect in relation to a warrant issued under this section as they have effect in relation to a warrant issued under that section.

*CAP. 156.*

(6) A person is not subject to the Immigration Act, in respect of his entry into or presence in Belize in pursuance of a warrant under this section, but if the warrant ceases to have effect while he is still in Belize he shall be treated for the purposes of that Act as if he has then illegally entered Belize.

(7) This section applies to a person detained in custody in a foreign State in consequence of being transferred there–

(a) from Belize under legislation relating to the transfer of offenders; or

(b) from any other State under any similar provision or arrangement;

as it applies to a person detained as mentioned in sub–section (1).

(8) Subject to sub–section (9), a person in Belize pursuant to a request made under this section–

(a) shall not be prosecuted, detained, punished or subjected to any other restriction of his personal liberty, in Belize, in respect of acts, omissions or convictions prior to his departure from the State to which the request was made;

(b) may refuse to answer any question or produce any document or article if the refusal is based on the laws of Belize; and

shall not be compelled to give or provide evidence, information or assistance for the purposes of, or in connection with, any criminal matter other than that to which the request relates.

(9) Paragraphs (8)(a) and (c) do not apply to a person who–

(a) has been officially informed by the central authority that his presence is no longer required for the purpose of the request and who voluntarily remains in Belize for more than fifteen days after the first date on which he had a reasonable opportunity to leave Belize; or

(b) having left Belize, returns otherwise than pursuant to the same or another request made under this section.

*Search, etc. for*

*18.–(1)*

*material relevant to overseas investigation.*

If, on an application made by a police officer, a Magistrate is satisfied–

(a) that criminal proceedings for an offence have been instituted against a person in a foreign State or that a person has been arrested in the course of a criminal investigation carried on in that State into such an offence; and

(b) that there are reasonable grounds for suspecting that there is on premises in Belize occupied or controlled by that person evidence relating to that offence;

he may issue a warrant authorising a police officer to enter and search those premises and to seize any such evidence found

*there.*

(2) The power to search conferred by sub–section (1) is only a power to search to the extent that is reasonably required for the purpose of discovering such evidence as is there mentioned.

(3) No application for a warrant or order shall be made by virtue of sub–section (1) except in pursuance of a direction given by the central authority in response to a request received–

(a) from a court or tribunal exercising criminal jurisdiction in the overseas State in question or a prosecuting authority in that State; or

(b) from any other authority in that State which appears to him to have the function of making request for the purposes of this section,

and any evidence seized by a police officer by virtue of this section shall be furnished by him to the central authority for transmission to that court, tribunal or authority.

(4) If in order to comply with the request it is necessary for any such evidence to be accompanied by any certificate, affidavit or other verifying document, the police officer shall also furnish for transmission such document of that nature as may be specified in the direction given by the central authority.

(5) Where the evidence consists of a document, the original or a copy shall be transmitted, and where it consists of any other article, the article itself or a description, photograph or other representation of it shall be transmitted, as may be necessary in order to comply with the request.

*Enforcement of overseas forfeiture orders.*

**19.** (1) The central authority may, by order published in the Gazette, provide for the enforcement in Belize of any order which–

is made by a court in a foreign State designated for the purposes of this section by the order; and

(b) is for the forfeiture and destruction, or the forfeiture and other disposal, of anything in respect of which an offence to which this section applies has been committed, or which was used in connection with the commission of such an offence.

(2) Without prejudice to the generality of sub–section (1), an order by the central authority under this section may provide for the registration by a court in Belize of any order of a court in a foreign State as a condition of its enforcement, and prescribe requirements to be satisfied before an order can be registered.

(3) An order by the central authority under this section may include such supplementary and incidental provisions as appear to the central authority to be necessary or expedient, and may apply for the purposes of the order, with such modifications as appear to the central authority to be appropriate, any provisions relating to confiscation or forfeiture orders under any other law.

(4) An order by the central authority under this section may make different provision for different cases.

(5) This section applies to any offence which corresponds

*CAP. 103.*

to or is similar to an offence under the Misuse of Drugs Act.

Specific Forms of Assistance

*Power to require information.*

**20.** (1) The central authority may, in response to a request for testimony or evidence to be given by a person in Belize, by notice in writing served on such person in Belize, require the person to provide the information relating to the testimony or

evidence before a court nominated by the central authority as provided in section 14(2).

(2) The provisions of section 15(2) shall, with the necessary modifications, apply to a request under this section.

(3) Where a request for assistance by a requesting State so stipulates–

(a) the court shall obtain the information sought by the request in the form of depositions of witnesses; or

(b) the court may require that original documents or copies of original documents be certified or authenticated,

*Schedule I.*

and in each case the Schedule I shall have effect.

*Duty to provide information.*

**21.** (1) A person on whom a notice has been served under section 20 shall appear before the court and provide the information specified in the notice, within the period specified in it.

(2) The court may extend the time specified in the notice served by the central authority if, in its opinion, the circumstances warrant.

*Location or identification of persons or items.*

**22.** (1) Subject to sub–section (2), the central authority may consider a request to assist in locating, or identifying and locating–

(a) a person who is believed to be in Belize;

(b) item or items, as specified in the request, believed to be in Belize.

(2) Where, on receipt of a request under sub–section (1), the central authority is satisfied–

(a) that the request relates to a criminal matter in the requesting State; and

(b) that there are reasonable grounds for believing that the person or item to whom the request relates–

(i) is or might be concerned in, or could give or provide evidence or assistance relevant to, the criminal matter; and

(ii) is in Belize,

the central authority may authorise, in writing, assistance in accordance with this section.

(3) The central authority may authorise the giving of assistance under sub–section (2) by forwarding the request to the police, public officer or other appropriate Government department in Belize, and the person or department requested shall use its best endeavours to locate or, as the case may be, identify and locate the person or item specified in the request, and shall report to the central authority the outcome.

(4) On receipt of a report referred to in sub–section (3), the central authority shall inform the requesting State of the result of the inquiries made pursuant to the request as provided in section 27.

*Transfer of persons in custody.*

**23.** (1) Subject to the provisions of this section, the central authority may, in response to a request, authorise the transfer of a person in custody in Belize to the territory of the requesting State under such terms and conditions as he may deem appropriate.

(2) The provisions of section 16, with the exception of sub– section (2), shall apply, with the necessary modifications, to a transfer of a person in custody in Belize to the territory of the requesting State under this section.

(3) No warrant shall be issued pursuant to section 16 for the purposes of this section in respect of a person in custody unless–

(a) the person has consented, in writing, to being transferred in accordance with the request referred to in sub–section (1) and the consent has been communicated to the central authority and to the requesting State;

(b) the central authority has consented to the transfer in writing and has communicated his consent to the person in custody and to the requesting State; and

(c) the requesting State has communicated its acceptance and observance of the terms and conditions of the transfer to the central authority and to the person in custody.

(4) For the purposes of sub–section (3)(a), consent may be given either–

(a) by the person in custody himself; or

(b) in circumstances in which it appears to the central authority inappropriate, by reason of the physical or mental condition or youth of such person in custody, for him to act for himself, by a person appearing to the central authority to be an appropriate person to act on his behalf,

but a consent once given is not capable of being withdrawn after the issue of the warrant.

*Transit of persons in custody.*

**24.** (1) The central authority may, in response to a request, authorise the transit through Belize of a person held in custody by the requesting State or a third State, whose personal appearance has been requested by the requesting State for purposes connected with a criminal investigation, prosecution or proceeding.

(2) The person in custody who has been authorised to transit through Belize under sub–section (1), who is being transported in an aircraft or ship which lands or calls at a place in Belize, shall be kept in such custody while in transit in Belize as the central authority may, in consultation with the Minister responsible for national security, direct in writing until his transportation is continued.

(3) Where–

(a) a person is being held in custody pursuant to a direction under sub–section (2); and

the person’s transportation is not, in the (b)

opinion of the central authority, continued within a reasonable time,

the central authority may, in consultation with the Minister responsible for national security, direct in writing that the person be transported in custody to the State from which the person was first transported, and such a direction shall be sufficient authority for that person’s removal from Belize by such means as the central authority directs.

## PART III

The Vienna Convention Controlled Drugs

*Interpretation.*

**29.** In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires–

“Class A drug”, “Class B drug” and “Class C drug” have the

*CAP. 103.*

same meaning specified in section 4 of the Misuse of Drugs Act;

*CAP. 103.*

“controlled drug” has the same meaning as in the Misuse of Drugs Act;

“drug trafficking offence” has the same meaning as in the Misuse of Drugs Act;

*CAP. 103.*

“enforcement officer” includes a police officer, customs officer, and member of the Belize Defence Force.

*Restrictions. CAP. 103.*

**30.** The Misuse of Drugs Act, has effect in relation to the restrictions on importation, exportation, production, supply and possession of controlled drugs and substances useful for manufacture of controlled drugs.

Offences at Sea

*Offences on Belizean ships.*

**31.** Anything which would constitute a drug trafficking offence if done on land in Belize shall constitute that offence if done on a Belizean ship.

*Ships used for illicit traffic.*

**32.** (1) This section applies to a Belizean ship, a ship registered in a Convention State or a ship not registered in any State.

(2) A person commits an offence if, on a ship to which this section applies, wherever it may be, he–

(a) has a controlled drug in his possession; or

(b) is in any way knowingly concerned in the carrying or concealing of a controlled drug on the ship,

knowing or having reasonable grounds to suspect that the drug is intended to be imported, or has been exported, contrary to

*CAP. 103.*

section 5 of the Misuse of Drugs Act, or to the law of a Convention State.

(3) A certificate purporting to be issued by or on behalf of the government of any Convention State to the effect that the

importation or exportation of a controlled drug is prohibited by the law of that State shall be evidence of the matters stated.

(4) A person who commits an offence under this section is liable–

(a) in a case where the controlled drug is a Class A drug–

(i) on summary conviction, to imprisonment for ten years or a fine of one hundred thousand dollars or to both, or

(ii) on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for fourteen years or an unlimited fine or to both;

(b) in a case where the controlled drug is a Class B drug–

(i) on summary conviction, to imprisonment for five years or a fine of fifty thousand dollars or to both, or

(ii) on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for seven years or a fine of two hundred and fifty thousand dollars or to both; and

(c) in a case where the controlled drug is a Class C drug–

(i) on summary conviction, to imprisonment for two years or a fine of twenty five thousand dollars or to both, or

on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for five years or a fine of one hundred thousand dollars or to both.

(5) An offence under this section shall be included in the

*CAP. 103.*

offences to which section 51 of the Misuse of Drugs Act applies.

*Enforcement powers. Schedule II.*

(b) authorised Belize to act for that purpose.

**34.** (1) Subject to this section, a Convention State may, under an agreement made or which may be made by the Government of Belize, exercise in relation to a ship in the territorial waters of Belize, powers corresponding to those conferred on enforcement officers by the Schedule II.

## PART IV

Miscellaneous

*Non–disclosure of confidential requests for assistance.*

**36.** (1) Unless otherwise authorized by law, a person who, because of his or her official capacity or office, and being aware of the confidential nature of the request, has knowledge of–

(a) the contents of a request;

(b) the fact that such request has been, or is about to be, made; or

(c) the fact that a request has been granted or refused,

shall not disclose such contents or fact except to the extent that the disclosure is necessary to execute the request.

(2) When requiring a person to provide a statement, testimony, information or evidence under this Act, the Magistrate, Judge or central authority may also, by the same instrument, direct the person providing a statement or testimony, or the custodian of evidence or information being provided, to keep confidential the fact of having given such statement or testimony, or having produced such evidence or information.

(3) A person who contravenes sub–section (1) or a direction under sub–section (2) commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding fifty thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months, or to both.

*Expenses.*

**37.** Any expenses incurred by the central authority under this Act shall be defrayed out of money provided by the National Assembly.

*Power to administer oaths.*

**2.** The court may in the proceedings take evidence on oath.

*Privileges of witnesses.*

(4) Without prejudice to sub–section (1), a person is not compelled under this Schedule to give any evidence if his doing so would be prejudicial to the security of Belize; and a certificate signed by or on behalf of the central authority acting in his discretion to the effect that it would be so prejudicial for that person to do so is conclusive evidence of that fact.

(3) Where the evidence consists of a document, the original or a copy shall be transmitted and, where it consists of another article, the article itself or a description, photograph or other representation of it shall be transmitted, as may be necessary in order to comply with the request.

## SCHEDULE II

### MUTUAL LEGAL ASSISTANCE AND INTERNATIONAL CO–OPERATION ACT

Enforcement Powers in Respect of Ships

[section 33]

Preliminary

*Interpretation.*

**1.** In this Schedule–

“enforcement officer” means–

(a) a customs officer, police officer or member of the Belize Defence Force; or

(b) any other person of a description specified in an order made for the purposes of this Schedule by the central authority; and

“ship” means the ship in relation to which the powers conferred by this Schedule are exercised.

*Power to stop, board, divert and detain.*

**2.** (1) An enforcement officer may stop the ship, board it and, if he thinks it necessary for the exercise of his functions, require it to be taken to a port in Belize and detain it there.

(2) Where an enforcement officer is exercising his powers with the authority of the central authority given under section 33(2) of the Act, the officer may require the ship to be taken to a port in the Convention State in question or, if that State has so requested, in any other State willing to receive it.

(3) For any of those purposes, he may require the master or any member of the crew to take such action as may be necessary.

(4) If an enforcement officer detains a vessel, he shall serve on the master a notice in writing stating that it is to be detained until the notice is withdrawn by the service on him of a further notice in writing signed by an enforcement officer.

*Power to search and obtain information.*

**3.** (1) An enforcement officer may search the ship, anyone on it and anything on it including its cargo.

(2) An enforcement officer may require any person on the ship to give information concerning himself or anything on the ship.

(3) Without prejudice to the generality of those powers, an enforcement officer may–

(a) open any containers;

(b) make tests and take samples of anything on the ship;

(c) require the production of documents, books or records relating to the ship or anything on it; or

(d) make photographs or copies of anything whose production he has power to require.

*Powers in respect of suspected offence.*

**4.** If an enforcement officer has reasonable grounds to suspect that an offence mentioned in section 31 or 32 has been committed on a ship to which that section applies, he may–

(a) arrest without warrant anyone whom he has reasonable grounds for suspecting to have committed the offence; and

(b) seize and detain anything found on the ship which appears to him to be evidence of the offence.

*Assistants.*

**5.** (1) An enforcement officer may take with him, to assist him in exercising his powers–

(a) any other persons; and

(b) any equipment or materials.

(2) A person whom an enforcement officer takes with him to assist him may perform any of the officer’s functions but only under the officer’s supervision.

*Use of reasonable force.*

**6.** An enforcement officer may use reasonable force, if necessary, in the performance of his functions.

*Evidence of authority.*

**7.** An enforcement officer shall, if required, produce evidence of his authority.

*Protection of officers.*

**8.** An enforcement officer shall not be liable in any civil or criminal proceedings for anything done in the purported performance of his functions under this Schedule if the court is satisfied that the act was done in good faith and that there were reasonable grounds for doing it.

*Offences.*

**9.** (1) A person commits an offence if he–

(a) intentionally obstructs an enforcement officer in the performance of any of his functions under this Schedule;

(b) fails without reasonable excuse to comply with a requirement made by an enforcement officer in the performance of those functions; or

in purporting to give information required by an enforcement officer for the performance of those functions–

(i) makes a statement which he knows to be false in a material particular or recklessly makes a statement which is false in a material particular; or

(ii) intentionally fails to disclose any material particular.

(2) A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on summary conviction to a fine of fifty thousand dollars.
